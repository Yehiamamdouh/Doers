<p>"How much does a campaign cost?" is the first question most clients ask, and the honest answer is: it depends on what the campaign has to achieve. Two brands can spend very different amounts on campaigns that look similar, because the cost is decided by a handful of factors, not by a price list.</p>

<p>This guide explains those factors, so you can plan a realistic budget and judge any proposal you receive from an <a href="/">advertising agency in Egypt</a>.</p>

<h2>1. The objective sets the size of the job</h2>

<p>A campaign that has to make a new brand known across Egypt needs reach: many people, many times. A campaign that has to bring B2B leads from a few hundred decision makers needs precision, not reach. The first is usually a media-heavy budget; the second spends more on targeting, content and follow-up. Agree on the objective and how it will be measured before you discuss money.</p>

<h2>2. Media is usually the biggest line</h2>

<p>For most campaigns, buying the space costs more than making the ads. What moves the media budget:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Channel:</strong> <a href="/tv-advertising/">TV</a> and prime <a href="/outdoor-advertising-egypt/">outdoor locations</a> reach millions but need bigger budgets; <a href="/digital-marketing-egypt-cairo/">digital</a> and <a href="/radio-advertising-egypt/">radio</a> can start smaller and scale.</li>
<li><strong>Season:</strong> Ramadan, back-to-school and the end of the year are the most expensive periods on TV and outdoor. See our guide to <a href="/ramadan-tv-advertising-egypt-budget/">planning a Ramadan TV budget</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Weight:</strong> how many people you want to reach, and how often. Too little weight is the most common way to waste money: the campaign runs, but not enough to be noticed.</li>
<li><strong>Location and timing:</strong> a billboard on a main bridge, a spot in a top drama series or a search keyword with heavy competition all cost more than quieter options.</li>
<li><strong>Negotiation:</strong> packages, bonus spots and long-term deals can change the real cost a lot. This is where an agency's relationships matter.</li>
</ul>

<h2>3. Production: making the ads</h2>

<p>Production covers everything you see and hear: films, photos, designs, copy and animation. What moves it:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Format:</strong> a TV commercial with actors, locations and post-production is a different scale of job from a set of social media designs.</li>
<li><strong>Talent:</strong> celebrities and influencers add reach and credibility, and a significant fee.</li>
<li><strong>Versions:</strong> one shoot can produce TV spots, short social cuts, outdoor visuals and stills. Planning all the versions at once costs far less than shooting again later. See our <a href="/media-production-egypt/">media production</a>.</li>
</ul>

<h2>4. Events and exhibitions: scale, build and venue</h2>

<p>For <a href="/event-management-cairo-egypt/">events</a> and <a href="/booth-production-egypt/">exhibition stands</a>, the cost follows what people will see and touch: the size of the space, custom structures, screens and lighting, staging, catering and the venue itself. A modular stand used at several shows a year costs less per show than a new custom stand each time. Our <a href="/exhibition-stand-egypt-planning-guide/">exhibition stand guide</a> lists the cost drivers in detail.</p>

<h2>5. Brand and strategy: the part that makes everything else work</h2>

<p>Strategy, research and <a href="/branding-agency-egypt/">branding</a> are usually a small share of the total, but they decide whether the rest of the budget works. A clear brand and message make every ad more effective, so the same media money delivers more.</p>

<h2>6. Agency fees</h2>

<p>Agencies charge in different ways: a fee per project, a monthly retainer, a commission on media, or a mix. None is better in itself. What matters is that you know exactly what you pay for, who owns the ad accounts and the work, and how results will be reported.</p>

<h2>How to get more from the same budget</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Fewer channels, enough weight.</strong> Two channels done properly beat five done thinly.</li>
<li><strong>Plan production once.</strong> Shoot for every format you will need.</li>
<li><strong>Book early.</strong> The best positions and prices go first, especially before Ramadan.</li>
<li><strong>Measure and move money monthly.</strong> Shift budget towards what is delivering against your KPIs.</li>
<li><strong>Use one team.</strong> When strategy, creative, media and production sit together, nothing is paid for twice.</li>
</ul>
