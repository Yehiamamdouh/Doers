<p>Ask five branding companies in Egypt for a quote and you may get five very different numbers. That is rarely because one is cheap and another is greedy. It is because each one is quoting a different job: a logo, a full identity, or a brand built from strategy up. This guide explains what decides the cost of branding in Egypt, what each level of work should include, and how to compare proposals fairly.</p>

<h2>First, what are you actually buying?</h2>

<p>"Branding" covers very different scopes. Before you compare prices, make sure every agency is quoting the same thing:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Logo design:</strong> a mark and a few colour and layout versions. Useful for a small business that needs to start, but it is not a brand system.</li>
<li><strong>Visual identity:</strong> logo plus colours, typography, graphic elements, photography style and templates, so every design looks like the same company.</li>
<li><strong>Full brand:</strong> research and positioning, the brand story and message, naming if needed, the visual identity, brand guidelines, and the first applications such as stationery, social templates, signage and packaging.</li>
<li><strong>Rebrand or refresh:</strong> updating an existing brand without losing the recognition you have built. This needs an audit of what works today before anything changes.</li>
</ul>

<h2>What decides the cost of branding in Egypt</h2>

<h3>1. Strategy and research</h3>
<p>Workshops with your team, interviews with customers, and a review of competitors take time from senior people, and they are what make the identity right rather than just pretty. A proposal without any strategy phase is cheaper because it skips the thinking.</p>

<h3>2. Naming</h3>
<p>Creating a new name means generating options, checking how they sound in Arabic and English, checking domains and social handles, and doing a first trademark search. If you already have a name, this cost disappears.</p>

<h3>3. The number of design routes and revisions</h3>
<p>Some agencies present one direction, others three. More routes and more revision rounds mean more design time. Ask exactly how many are included and what happens if you need more.</p>

<h3>4. The identity system and guidelines</h3>
<p>A complete brand book explains how to use the logo, colours, fonts, imagery and tone, with examples. It is what lets your team, printers and future suppliers use the brand correctly without calling the agency every time. A short PDF with a logo on a few backgrounds is a different, smaller job.</p>

<h3>5. Applications</h3>
<p>Every item the brand is applied to adds work: business cards and stationery, social media templates, a website look, uniforms, vehicles, <a href="/signage-internal-branding-egypt/">signage and office branding</a>, and <a href="/booth-production-egypt/">exhibition stands</a>. Packaging is usually the largest single item, because each product and size needs its own layout and print-ready files.</p>

<h3>6. Bilingual work</h3>
<p>In Egypt most brands need to work in Arabic and English. A logo and type system that are designed for both from the start cost more than an English-only identity, and much less than fixing the Arabic later.</p>

<h3>7. Timing</h3>
<p>A tight deadline, such as a launch date or a trade show, means more people working at once. Plan branding early, before media and production, so it does not become the bottleneck. Our <a href="/product-launch-plan-egypt/">product launch plan</a> shows where branding fits.</p>

<h2>How to compare branding quotes</h2>

<ul>
<li>Put the proposals side by side and list exactly what each one delivers: strategy, naming, routes, revisions, guidelines, applications.</li>
<li>Check who owns the final files and the rights to the logo. The answer should be you, with all source files delivered.</li>
<li>Ask to see a full brand book from a past project, not just logo images.</li>
<li>Ask who will work on your brand and how much of the work is done in-house.</li>
<li>Check the timeline, and what happens if approvals on your side take longer.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Can I start with a logo and build the rest later?</h3>
<p>Yes, if the logo is designed as part of a system that can grow. Ask for a basic identity (colours, fonts, usage rules) with the logo, so later work stays consistent.</p>

<h3>How long does branding take?</h3>
<p>It depends on scope and on how quickly decisions are made. We plan around your launch date and agree a timeline with clear approval points from the start.</p>

<h3>Do we need a brand book?</h3>
<p>If more than one person or supplier will use your brand, yes. Without guidelines the brand slowly drifts, and fixing it later costs more than writing the rules once.</p>
