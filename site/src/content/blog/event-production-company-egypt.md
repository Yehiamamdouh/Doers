<p>A conference, a product launch, a gala dinner or a brand activation can look effortless on the night. Behind it are weeks of planning, dozens of suppliers and a team that has thought about every minute. That team is the event production company. This guide explains what an event production company in Egypt actually does, how the work runs from the first brief to the last guest leaving, and what to ask before you hire one.</p>

<h2>Event management vs event production</h2>

<p>The two terms overlap. Event management usually means the whole job: objectives, guest list, invitations, logistics and follow-up. Event production is the part that makes the experience real: the concept, the stage, the screens, the sound and lighting, the build, the run of show and the technical crew on the night. A good agency does both under one team, so the idea and its delivery never drift apart. For the wider picture, see <a href="/what-do-event-management-companies-do/">what event management companies do</a>.</p>

<h2>What an event production company handles</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Concept and creative:</strong> the idea of the evening, the look, the story the event tells and how guests move through it.</li>
<li><strong>Venue:</strong> finding and negotiating the hotel, hall or outdoor space, permits, and a site visit with the technical team.</li>
<li><strong>Stage and set design:</strong> stage size, backdrops, branded arches, photo walls and entrance experiences, drawn in 3D before anything is built.</li>
<li><strong>Audio-visual:</strong> LED screens, projection, sound, lighting, live streaming and recording.</li>
<li><strong>Build and installation:</strong> fabricating sets and branding, then installing them in the venue, often overnight.</li>
<li><strong>Content:</strong> opening films, presentations, motion graphics and on-screen visuals.</li>
<li><strong>Talent and entertainment:</strong> hosts, speakers, bands and performers, with their technical riders.</li>
<li><strong>Run of show:</strong> a minute-by-minute script that tells everyone what happens when, plus rehearsals.</li>
<li><strong>Show day:</strong> a stage manager, technical crew and hosts on site, and a plan B for the things that go wrong.</li>
</ul>

<h2>How the work runs, step by step</h2>

<ol>
<li><strong>Brief:</strong> the objective, audience, number of guests, date, city and budget range.</li>
<li><strong>Concept and proposal:</strong> one or more creative directions with a first layout and a cost breakdown.</li>
<li><strong>Venue and design:</strong> site visit, 3D renders of the stage and spaces, and approval.</li>
<li><strong>Production:</strong> building sets and branding, preparing content, booking AV and talent.</li>
<li><strong>Rehearsals:</strong> a technical run and a full rehearsal with speakers and hosts.</li>
<li><strong>Show day:</strong> installation, final checks, the event itself, and dismantling.</li>
<li><strong>Wrap-up:</strong> photos and video for your channels, attendance numbers and lessons for next time.</li>
</ol>

<h2>How much time to allow</h2>

<p>The bigger the build, the earlier you should start. A dinner or seminar in a hotel can come together in a few weeks. A launch with a custom stage, LED screens and filmed content needs more time for design approvals and fabrication. In Egypt, also plan around Ramadan, public holidays and the big trade shows, when venues and crews are in demand. Our <a href="/egypt-advertising-calendar/">Egypt advertising and exhibitions calendar</a> shows the busy months, and our <a href="/product-launch-plan-egypt/">product launch plan</a> shows where the event fits in a wider campaign.</p>

<h2>Questions to ask an event production company</h2>

<ul>
<li>Which parts do you produce in-house, and which do you subcontract?</li>
<li>Can we see 3D renders before anything is built?</li>
<li>Who is our stage manager on the night, and how many crew will be on site?</li>
<li>What is the plan if the power, internet or a speaker fails?</li>
<li>Who handles permits, venue rules and safety?</li>
<li>What content will we receive after the event, and when?</li>
</ul>

<h2>Getting more from the event</h2>

<p>An event lasts one evening, but its content can feed your channels for weeks. Plan the photos, short videos and interviews before the day, and agree how the event will be measured: attendance, meetings booked, leads captured, coverage. If the event is part of a trade show, combine it with your <a href="/booth-production-egypt/">exhibition stand</a> so the message is the same in the hall and on stage.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Do you work outside Cairo?</h3>
<p>Yes. Many events in Egypt happen in Cairo, the North Coast and Red Sea resorts. The production team travels with the build and crew.</p>

<h3>Can you work with our venue's in-house suppliers?</h3>
<p>Yes. Some hotels require their own AV or catering. We coordinate with them and fill the gaps, so you still have one team responsible for the result.</p>

<h3>How is the cost of an event decided?</h3>
<p>By the number of guests, the venue, the size of the build, the screens and technical setup, the content and the talent. We send a detailed breakdown with the proposal, so you can see where the budget goes.</p>
